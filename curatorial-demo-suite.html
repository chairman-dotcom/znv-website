Curatorial Practice — Selected Work

Art is nothing
without the viewer.

A curator's practice built on three questions: what a work is made of, whom it was made for, and who finds it afterward — and why that third, uninvited audience is where meaning keeps happening.

Framework

Three aspects, three communities

Every object is read through what it's made of, how it moves through the world, and what it's come to mean — and every object belongs, whether it likes it or not, to three overlapping communities. The case studies below are that framework in practice, not just theory.

01 — FORMALISM

What it's made from

Material, composition, craft — the object on its own terms.

02 — SOCIETY

How it moves in the world

The mechanisms it passes through: markets, institutions, audiences, geography.

03 — THEORY

What it comes to mean

Interpretation and reinterpretation — meaning as something made, not just found.

The most interesting community is the one the artist never intended — the critics and historians who arrive later and bring their own speculation to the object, engaging its psychic value as spectators rather than owners of its truth. Framework developed under Prof. Cecilia González-Andrieu — where art and religion intersect, and theology becomes a tool for reading meaning.

LMU.CO-OP — FOUNDING MEMBER → PRESIDENT
Building a gallery from the ground up
Stewardship & Facilities Leadership

Joined a brand-new student gallery co-op as its very first member, after years of student fundraising finally built LMU a dedicated gallery wing. Elected Vice President, later President.

Held sole responsibility for keys and access, and for the stewardship of every object in the space — housing, storage, and wall maintenance. Installed with mobile wall systems and enforced the gallery's 56-inch hanging standard across every show. Demonstrates: aspect 02, society — the physical mechanisms an object has to pass through before anyone ever sees it.
Original study — gallery walls & mobile partitions
LMU × OTIS — INAUGURAL PARTNER EXHIBITION
First show curating outside the family
Selection & Artist Care

Curator-photographer Molly Corey led a yearly practicum pairing LMU's gallery with Otis College of Art & Design. This was the first time curating artists who weren't peers.

Selected Charlie Edminston, a colorblind artist who built a colorblindness-test artwork he couldn't fully see himself, relying on others to choose the paint — his frames were later damaged in a mounting accident and had to be repaired at real cost; he went on to design skateboards for MTV. Also selected Franchesca Quintano, whose underwater imagery was left uncollected long enough that the gallery instituted formal pickup contracts — she's since become a working muralist. Demonstrates: aspect 01, formalism — a work whose entire material logic (color) was inaccessible to its own maker.
Original studies — spectrum dot field / underwater current
DENVER — NAGARAJA, SOLO EXHIBITION
Negotiating for the artist, not the wall
Artist Representation & Contract Negotiation

Curated several exhibitions with visionary digital artist Android Jones, including Nagaraja, his first solo show. Toured the Santa Fe Art District with the gallery's director to place the work.

Pushed back on a proposed contract claiming 50% of any future sale of unsold work, indefinitely, even through other venues — negotiated down to a fixed window instead, a harder case given a mix of print editions and unique drawings on the wall. Later edited and effectively ghostwrote the artist's statement, framing his digital tools as "minerals" — a literal parallel between the metal implant from his own brain surgery and the mineral basis of both electronics and paint pigment. Demonstrates: aspect 02 and 03 together — market mechanism and theoretical meaning, negotiated in the same conversation.
Original study — visionary mandala, concentric form
ONGOING — BRONZE SCULPTURE SERIES
Scaling a studio practice into an exhibition
Producer & Wall Text Writer

Collaborating with James Harris — former lead photographer for Design Miami — on bronze sculpture built from high-resolution leaf photography, composited and produced through precision 3D printing before casting.

Proposed exhibiting the finished bronzes on plinths of petrified wood — now a signature of the presentation. Currently building the client and investor base to scale the work for exhibition at Chatsworth House's annual outdoor sculpture show, alongside writing wall texts and exhibition PDFs. Demonstrates: aspect 01, formalism — a material born as a photograph, ending as bronze.
Original study — cast leaf forms on petrified wood
LONDON — TRAVELING SOLO EXHIBITION
Writing meaning into the catalogue
Catalogue Essayist

Authored the essay for Sleeprs — an anonymous, masked performance artist — for his London solo show, printed as part of the exhibition's traveling catalogue.

Writing for an artist who withholds his own identity is its own curatorial problem: the essay had to carry meaning the artist himself wasn't going to explain in person. Demonstrates: aspect 03, theory — interpretation standing in for a maker who deliberately isn't present.
Original study — the mask, unlit
DENVER — REMAX OFFICE ART PROGRAM
A gallery inside a real estate office
Corporate & Community Placement

As office manager, built a rotating art exhibition into the office's existing client events. Met wildlife painter Arturo Garcia while volunteering at the Denver Art Society, an artist-run cooperative gallery.

Garcia left a prior career after a cancer diagnosis to paint full-time, working in abstract realism focused on capturing movement in wildlife. Chosen for the program for the sincerity of that commitment — art for its own sake. Also represents chalk artist Bryce, whose large-scale ephemeral chalk work and commercial branding jobs (like a brewery's chalkboards) support his family; advised him on entering the gallery market. Demonstrates: aspect 02, society — an exhibition built inside a business that had never held one before.
Original studies — motion brushwork / chalk dust

SAMPLE LESSON PLAN — NOT AN ACTUAL SCHEDULED CLASS

Held in Metal: Process, Material, and the Nonhuman

Course: Mechanical Engineering — Materials & Manufacturing Processes Object: bronze sculpture (leaf photography → 3D print → cast bronze) Format: 75-min gallery + print study room visit
Before the visit

Faculty meeting & goal-setting

+
Meet with the instructor to learn what the course already covers about casting, additive manufacturing, and material transformation — then set one goal for the visit: can students trace a single decision (why bronze, why this scale) all the way from source photograph to final object?
  • Confirm which object comes out of storage, coordinated with the Collections Manager
  • Send a one-page pre-visit reading: a short note on the leaf-photograph-to-bronze process
During the visit

Object-based inquiry in the gallery

+
Students spend the first ten minutes looking before being told anything — a core inquiry-model move. Then the conversation opens with observation, not information.
"Before I tell you what this is made of — what do you think this surface is telling you about how it was made?"
  • Reveal the process in stages: photograph → digital composite → print → cast — pausing for questions at each step
  • Small groups examine a physical 3D-print test piece alongside the finished bronze, comparing surface and weight
"What does the material lose, and what does it gain, moving from a photograph of something living to a cast metal object?"
After the visit

Follow-up assignment

+
A short written or sketched response: students choose one material in their own coursework or lab and describe what changes — physically and conceptually — when it moves from raw form to finished object, using the same before/after framing from the gallery.
  • Optional extension: an artmaking activity — a small cast or 3D-printed test piece — for courses with studio access
  • Faculty debrief scheduled the following week to fold observations back into the syllabus
